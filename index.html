Minecraft mod · Game analytics · Reinforcement learning

An AI fight coach that tells you why you lost the exchange.

Vertex is a Fabric mod for Minecraft 26.2. It records your sword fights from the exact signals the server sends, replays them in 3D, and coaches every exchange in plain English: what happened, why, what to do instead, and why that works. Behind it is a self-play lab where RL bots train on real headless servers, and a research question: does what a bot learns actually transfer to people?

How it works

No input automation, no HUD during the fight, and nothing leaves your computer. The mod only listens to packets the client already receives.

  1. 1

    Record

    Read-only mixins on 4 packet handlers. Every tick: positions, velocity, your exact keys and attack charge, held items, armour, and the blocks around the fight.

    record/DuelRecorder
  2. 2

    Classify hits

    Each damage packet is paired with the server's crit animation and attack sounds, so CRIT / SPRINT_HIT / WEAK is the server's verdict, then checked against what your client predicted.

    record/CombatPackets
  3. 3

    Detect

    Hits are grouped into exchanges. 20 rule-based techniques fire on the tick data: jump resets, S-taps, failed jump-ins, whiff punishes, combos…

    core/detect/InteractionDetector
  4. 4

    Explain

    A prioritised explainer (mistakes before strengths) writes four-part coaching from the owner's own Sword guides, behind an interface so an AI explainer can replace it.

    core/explain/Explainer
  5. 5

    Replay & query

    A private void world rebuilds the recorded arena; ghosts act out the fight. An MCP server lets AI assistants query every replay with 7 tools.

    replay/ · mcp-server/

The AI lab: can a bot's skill teach a person?

A server-side mod spawns two real server-player bots on a headless Minecraft server, driven one decision at a time from Python through a lockstep socket protocol and a PettingZoo environment (9–19× real time). Policies train with PPO in a league (self-play, snapshots, scripted styles, Elo), kick-started by behaviour cloning and a curriculum, then distilled into an LSTM with an opponent-prediction head. Every run is tracked in MLflow with a champion gate.

Negative results, reported as such

No value head beats the health bar on the human's fights. The fight "memory" that seemed to read the player turned out to be measured against a wiped-memory baseline that was itself below simply guessing the player's most common keys (83.1%).

What does work

A player model fine-tuned from the bot's network predicts the human at 2.44 nats per decision vs 4.20 for frequencies (90.6% vs 80.6% of movement keys), and its habit report matches the player's real rates within 3 points.

Where it's going

A pre-registered study (fixed probe opponent, fixed metrics) on what makes self-play knowledge transfer to people, and LLM coaching whose advice is checked by counterfactual arena rollouts.

Pilot scale: one player, 7 traced duels, 35 fights. Intervals and caveats are in the project's results notes.

Engineering highlights

Architecture enforced by the build

The detector and explainer live in a core package with zero Minecraft imports. The MCP server compiles core without Minecraft on its classpath, so a stray import breaks the build. That's what keeps it unit-testable and reusable.

Verified against bytecode

Vanilla 26.2's crit and sprint rules, sound attribution and packet handlers were confirmed with javap on the real remapped jar before use. They weren't guessed from old wikis.

Latency-aware timing

Your hits are timestamped at your click, matched to the confirming packet, so replays line up with what you did, not your ping. When client and server disagree it's flagged as SERVER_DISAGREED.

Safe threading

All sampling happens on the main thread. Analysis runs on its own thread over an immutable trace and hands results back with Minecraft.execute. It never touches live game objects.

Reproducible ML

Versioned Parquet datasets, hash-locked fight-level splits (70/20/10), a champion gate with fight-resampled intervals, and an MLflow model registry with REST serving.

Fair bots

Bots see the opponent 200 ms late, like a real client, and play with vanilla client rules and physics. Knockback is applied the way a client applies the motion packet.
